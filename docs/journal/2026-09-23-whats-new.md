# 2026-09-23 — A changelog that cannot drift

Stage 5 of the studio guide plan: `/studio/whats-new`, on the version scheme
settled when the plan was approved — the date, and the workspace file version
current on that date.

## Why that scheme, and what makes it stick

The alternative was a product version, and there is no product version: every
package here is `0.1.0` or `0.0.0` and private, and the handover archive
already stamps itself with `apps/playground`'s `0.1.0`. A changelog headed
"v0.8" would have contradicted the number a client was holding.

A date and a schema version are both facts the repository has. But a fact
nobody checks is a number that drifts, so the newest entry is asserted against
`BLUEPRINT_WORKSPACE_FILE_VERSION`:

```
AssertionError: the format is at v9 and the newest entry says v8
```

That is the test bumping the constant to 9 and nothing else. It is the whole
argument for the scheme in one line — a format change without an entry beside
it now fails, on the day it happens rather than the day somebody notices the
changelog is a year old.

Three more hold the shape: every entry names a version this build can open,
the entries run newest first, and the version never rises as the date falls.

## Six entries, each anchored to a bump

The dates came out of `git log -S` on the version constant rather than out of
memory: versions 2 to 5 on 31 August, 6 and 7 on 6 September, 8 on 18
September. Everything else is journal entries with dates on them.

## And the correction the changelog was written to catch

Writing it turned up that **version 8 is not the button schemes.** It is
preview frames and layout uses moving to the root of the workspace document.
The button schemes arrived on 12 September without a bump at all.

That claim is in the journal entry for the reference-workspace upgrade, and in
the commit message under it, and I repeated it twice more in conversation. The
journal is corrected in place with a note saying what it used to say. The
commit message stands, because a commit message is a record of what was
believed at the time.

Worth sitting with: the mistake survived a plan, a pull request, a merge and
two rounds of review, and was caught by the first thing that had to _use_ the
fact rather than mention it. A number quoted in prose is a number nobody
checks.

## The badge is formatted in one place, with a locale

`en-GB` and `timeZone: "UTC"`, explicitly. These pages are rendered at build
time into an archive, so a build in another zone must not date an entry a day
earlier — which is exactly what an unqualified `new Date("2026-01-01")` gives
west of UTC. There is a test for that one too, because it is the kind of bug
that only appears on somebody else's machine.
